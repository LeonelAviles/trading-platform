# Automated-strategy benchmark: Topstep $50K Trading Combine

September 7, 2026. This replaces Apex as the research benchmark at the user's
request. No account was purchased, no trading connection was created, and no
orders were submitted. The original OR15 signal and exit rules remain frozen.

**Topstep supports the intended automation. The current OR15 candidate does not
yet demonstrate dependable evaluation performance.** Position sizing reduces
breaches, but the strategy's modest edge then takes too long or fails to reach
the profit objective within the tested horizons.

## Why this provider

Topstep's current API documentation explicitly permits custom bots through
TopstepX / ProjectX. Order execution must originate on the trader's personal
device; VPS, VPN and remote-server order transmission are prohibited. API access
is a separate subscription, and HFT remains prohibited. This fits local research
and eventual local automation of a once-per-day strategy.
[Official API policy](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/11187768-topstepx-api-access).

## What was tested

Take the frozen failed-breakout + delta + 3R candidate from the
[OR15 study](2026-09-07-or15.md). Reprice its two existing print replays at current
Topstep commissions: one-tick and two-tick adverse slippage, respectively.
Query adverse price excursions during each trade to check the loss floor while
the trade is open, before considering its eventual closing result.

Position-sizing alternatives, set before calculating these results:

- Original fixed one ES contract, as a diagnostic baseline.
- MES-sized proxies with maximum initial loss budgets of $100, $150 or $200.
  Each budget includes estimated stop slippage and full round-trip fees.
- Reduce the budget further to at most **10% of remaining distance to the
  loss floor**. Round contracts down; skip if even one micro exceeds the budget.
  Skip entries when remaining headroom is $250 or less. Never force a minimum
  one-contract trade or increase size to recover losses.
- Keep one trade per day, the original stop/target geometry, and 15:58 New York
  flatten. That is before the ordinary Topstep weekday deadline. Shortened
  sessions remain excluded as in the original study.

**MES limitation:** local data contain ES only. MES dollar returns and costs are
applied to ES execution prices for this sizing experiment. These are not actual
MES fills, and they do not validate MES liquidity, spreads or execution. A
production strategy could use ES signals and MES execution, but that combination
needs paired data and testing.

The evaluator reserves the whole round-trip fee during the open trade, slightly
more conservative than charging half at entry. The floor is static during each
session, updates after the close, and locks at starting balance. Best-day profit
survives subsequent losing days. No result passes merely because unrealized
profit touches the target. Closed profit and consistency must both qualify.

Test every available contiguous **20-session** and **60-session** start, giving
147 and 107 scenarios, respectively. These horizons are research comparisons,
not provider deadlines. All starts overlap heavily and use previously researched
data; their frequencies must not be advertised as independent pass probabilities.

## Results

One tick of adverse slippage; all current per-trade costs included:

| Sizing | 60-session starts | Met numerical pass rules | Breached loss floor | Still unfinished |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| One ES | 107 | 27 (25.2%) | 80 | 0 |
| MES proxy, $100 risk cap | 107 | 0 | 0 | 107 |
| MES proxy, $150 risk cap | 107 | 6 (5.6%) | 0 | 101 |
| MES proxy, $200 risk cap | 107 | 19 (17.8%) | 0 | 88 |

The $200 proxy passed in a median **40 eligible sessions among successful
scenarios**. That excludes unfinished and breached scenarios; it is not an
unconditional expected passing time. None of the micro-sized alternatives
passed within any of the 147 twenty-session windows. The one-ES baseline
passed 34, breached 104 and remained unfinished in nine of those shorter tests.

With two ticks of adverse slippage, the sixty-session outcome counts happened
to remain the same; individual P&L and paths still changed. This is not proof
of robustness: the single full-history start below is particularly sensitive.

Starting December 1 and continuing until pass, breach or July 31:

| Sizing | One-tick result | Two-tick result |
|---|---|---|
| One ES | Breached December 10 | Breached December 10 |
| MES proxy, $100 cap | Unfinished, +$930.58 | Unfinished, +$517.81 |
| MES proxy, $150 cap | Unfinished, +$639.86 | Unfinished, +$920.99 |
| MES proxy, $200 cap | Numerical pass May 19, +$3,239.67 | Unfinished July 31, +$592.68 |

Sizing is path-dependent: different costs change account headroom, contract
rounding, skipped trades and subsequent outcomes. Consequently a higher risk
cap does not imply proportionally higher P&L, nor must each individual stressed
run produce lower P&L. The original one-ES run breaches before its first large
recovery, despite being profitable over the unrestricted full-history study.

## Decision and next research requirement

Use **Topstep $50K on TopstepX** for subsequent automated-strategy research.
Judge candidates on reaching the target with consistency while protecting the
actual drawdown allowance, including unrealized losses. The $50K label is not
the amount available to lose.

The $100–$200 budgets are tested research controls, not an assertion that this
strategy is ready for deployment. The present candidate remains weak: smaller
positions preserved eligibility in this historical model but usually did not
reach the target. We need a stronger independently validated signal, paired MES
execution data, and then practice-account verification before declaring it
capable of reliably passing or sustaining a funded account.

Subscription, API and activation fees are not trading-account deductions in
this evaluator and are excluded from personal net economics. Long passing times
therefore have an additional cost not captured by reported trading profits.
