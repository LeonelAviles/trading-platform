# ES 15-minute opening range: findings, September 7, 2026

**The opening range provides useful context, but this experiment did not find a
credible high-profit-factor strategy.** The best development-selected rule with
enough trades was an order-flow-confirmed failed breakout, with a 3R target.
Its independent print replay produced PF 1.16 at a 29.8% win rate; the later
May–July diagnostic weakened to PF 1.06. These are simulated results, not live
returns or a platform validation verdict.

This is a bounded experiment, not evidence that every possible opening-range
strategy fails. It tests specific fixed stops, targets, entry hours, and filters.

## Data and research integrity

- ES, December 1, 2025–July 31, 2026: **166 complete RTH sessions**, 103 in
  December–April development and 63 in the May–July chronological diagnostic.
- 44 local-date groups excluded: 37 without preopen data (largely Sunday evening
  groups and closures), seven without a full 390-minute session. The latter have
  0–225 RTH bars. This is a full-session study; it does not cover shortened days.
- Contract chosen from each date's volume accumulated between UTC midnight and
  09:30 New York. The platform's full-day-volume front-month map is not used,
  since it incorporates later information. This is a causal liquidity rule,
  not necessarily the exchange's canonical roll convention.
- Exact trade VWAP, anchored at 09:30. Every retained minute's volume and signed
  aggressor delta reconciled to the underlying prints. Databento side B means
  buy aggression, A sell aggression; unknown side contributes no signed delta.
- **No pristine holdout exists here.** The earlier project notebook already
  examined December–July. The platform's nominal OOS label does not restore
  unseen status. This study leaves the existing split and app state unchanged.
- Exactly 40 configurations specified before this run: four entry families,
  five filters, two targets. Selection uses December–April PF, requiring at
  least 30 development trades. May–July does not select a replacement winner.
  The strategy still carries selection bias from these trials and prior work.

## What the levels tell us at 09:45

Freeze the high H, low L, open O and width W=H−L of **09:30 inclusive to 09:45
exclusive, America/New_York**. ET handles daylight saving; fixed EST does not.

Directional diagnostic: which gets touched first before noon, **H+0.5W** or
**L−0.5W**? There were 146 resolved sessions, 20 unresolved, and no same-minute
ambiguous outcomes. Accuracy below conditions on resolution; it is not the
probability of a successful trade on an arbitrary day.

| Information frozen at 09:45 | Correct direction | Observations |
|---|---:|---:|
| Opening close versus 09:30 open | 59.7% | 144 non-ties |
| Opening close versus opening VWAP | 63.7% | 146 |
| Sign of first-15-minute aggressor delta | 58.9% | 146 |
| Pick whichever extension is nearer to current price | 62.5% | 144 non-ties |
| Always predict the upper extension | 54.1% | 146 |

The 95% Wilson interval for the VWAP result is **55.6%–71.1%**, before adjustments
for inspecting multiple signals. On the identical 144 non-tie sessions, VWAP
scores 63.2% versus 62.5% for the nearer-boundary rule. A distance-only,
zero-drift continuous-price barrier reference implies about 64.9% accuracy for
the VWAP choices, although that illustrative model ignores the noon deadline.
These controls were added after the initial screen to investigate proximity;
they did not change trading rules or candidate selection.

**Interpretation:** the apparent advantage over 50/50 is substantially explained
by price already being closer to one boundary. This does not demonstrate that
VWAP adds an exploitable predictive edge. Directional accuracy also ignores the
entry price, stop distance, and cost of reaching the target.

## Trade comparisons

All rules enter on the next minute after a completed signal, before 11:30;
one trade per day; stop distance max(4 ES points, half the opening width),
rounded up to a tick; 2R or 3R target; flatten at 15:58. Both directions are
allowed. The median opening width was 20.75 points.

Filters: none; price versus VWAP; signal-minute and trailing-three-minute delta
aligned with the proposed direction; VWAP plus delta; or both plus opening
volume at least its previous-20-session median, with a minimum ten-day history.

Bar screen uses one tick of adverse slippage on market entries and stop/forced
exits, **$4.50 round-trip commission**, and a target filled only after one tick
of trade-through. Both stop and target touched in one bar means stop first;
gap stops fill at the worse of the open/stop before slippage. Entry-bar exits
are included. One ES contract, no compounding, no account return assumptions.

| Rule | Target | All trades | Dec–Apr PF | May–Jul PF | Combined PF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Plain breakout | 2R | 164 | 0.89 | 0.93 | 0.90 |
| Plain breakout | 3R | 164 | 0.82 | 0.65 | 0.76 |
| Breakout + VWAP + delta + relative volume | 2R | 77 | 1.18 | 1.05 | 1.13 |
| Plain breakout retest | 3R | 142 | 0.91 | 0.86 | 0.89 |
| Failed breakout, no filter | 3R | 143 | 1.06 | 0.88 | 0.99 |
| **Failed breakout + delta** | **3R** | **131** | **1.22** | **1.06** | **1.16** |
| Open retest + VWAP | 3R | 18 | 4.51 | 0.93 | 2.01 |

The tempting PF 4.51 development result had **only eight trades** and failed the
minimum sample requirement; its next ten trades had PF 0.93. Reporting just
the combined 2.01 would be misleading. Only six of the 40 configurations had
combined PF above 1. Full results, including all losing trials, are in the CSV.

Adding VWAP alone left the plain breakout trades unchanged in this sample.
Adding VWAP to the selected failed-breakout rule worsened the result to combined
PF 0.97. More confirmations did not reliably improve profitability.

## The selected candidate, defined exactly

Research hypothesis: a failed attempt to leave the opening range, followed by
aggression back into it, sometimes starts a larger reversal.

1. Build and freeze the 15-minute range.
2. Starting with the 09:45 minute, wait for a completed minute to close outside
   H or L. This arms the corresponding breakout direction.
3. A subsequent minute must close back inside [L,H], immediately after an
   outside close. A return inside resets the breakout state even if no trade
   qualifies. Fade the breakout: short after the upper break fails, long after
   the lower break fails.
4. Both that return minute's aggressor delta and the sum of its last three
   minutes' deltas must agree with the fade. No VWAP filter in this candidate.
5. Enter at the first print at least 250 ms after the next-minute boundary,
   with one adverse tick. Last eligible signal is 11:28, entry before 11:30.
6. Stop max(4 points, W/2), rounded up to a tick, from actual slipped entry;
   target three times that distance. No trailing or breakeven adjustment.
   One trade per session; force exit at the first print at/after 15:58.

Print replay activates brackets on subsequent prints. Stop exits use the actual
triggering print plus adverse slippage; limit targets require trade-through and
receive their limit price. This checks sequencing and delayed entry, but it is
**not** an L3 queue/market-impact model or a NautilusTrader validation run.

| Print replay | Trades | Win rate | Profit factor | Net P&L | Max realized drawdown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dec–Apr development | 81 | 32.1% | 1.23 | $7,623 | $8,975 |
| May–Jul diagnostic | 50 | 26.0% | 1.06 | $1,237.50 | $5,600.50 |
| Combined | 131 | 29.8% | 1.16 | $8,860.50 | $8,975 |

Average winner / average loser: **2.74**. Average net trade: **$67.64**, falling
to $24.75 in May–July. Initial stop risk per one-contract trade ranged from
$200 to $1,362.50, median $525, before fees and stop slippage. Maximum observed
consecutive losing trades: eight. Drawdown uses closed daily P&L; intratrade
drawdown and actual capital/margin requirements are not estimated here.

Monthly net P&L: Dec −$2,439; Jan +$1,870; Feb −$5,621; Mar +$8,730.50;
Apr +$5,082.50; May +$2,202; Jun +$2,240.50; Jul −$3,205.

## How fragile is it?

- Double adverse slippage to two ticks on each market/stop fill: combined PF
  **1.13**, later-period PF **1.03**. This reruns fill geometry and outcomes.
- Remove the five largest winning trades: combined PF **0.89**, net **−$6,042**.
  Tail dependence is expected for low-win-rate systems; it still shows how
  little evidence of profitability remains if a few large wins are missed.
- A seeded 5,000-draw circular moving-block bootstrap of daily P&L, five days
  per block and including zero-trade days, yields a combined 95% PF interval
  **0.71–1.76**. Later-period interval: **0.39–2.03**. Neither excludes losses.
  These intervals are conditional on the selected rule and do not correct for
  strategy selection; they must not be read as evidence of significance.
- One instrument, eight months, only 131 trades; earlier research used these
  same months. No pristine unseen-data claim is justified.

**Decision:** keep the failed-breakout/delta rule as a research candidate. This
experiment does not support calling it a high-PF edge or deploying it. The next
meaningful test is the frozen rule on independently acquired, previously unused
ES periods spanning different conditions, followed by forward paper trading
and the platform's execution engine. Any rule changed after that test needs a
new untouched period. NQ would require its own data, costs and validation.

## External evidence and reproduction

A low win rate can coexist with profitability. Concretum's research reports
such a profile for a modified **five-minute QQQ** opening strategy. That is a
different instrument and specification; it does not establish an ES 15-minute
edge. [Author's research summary](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable).

A separate MNQ preprint evaluates common intraday momentum families under
cost and validation requirements and reports that none of those tested families
passes all its criteria. It is supporting context about testing discipline,
not proof about this ES rule. [Mesfin, revised July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004).

Trade/bar semantics are documented by the data provider:
[Databento schemas](https://databento.com/docs/schemas-and-data-formats).
